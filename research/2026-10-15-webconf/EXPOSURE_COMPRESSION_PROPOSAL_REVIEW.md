# Review: Does exposure policy change measured embedding-compression damage?

Review date: 16 October 2026. Status: methodological assessment, targeted literature screen and read-only support check. No new neural model, calibration experiment, dataset download, Kaggle operation or API inference was executed.

**Subsequent execution update:** the user-approved MF pilot and mathematical specification have now been completed in [EXPOSURE_MF_REPORT.md](D:/web_2/experiments/2026-10-16-exposure-mf/EXPOSURE_MF_REPORT.md). A small positive raw policy gap shrinks after intercept calibration; calibrated and ranking-gap intervals include zero. The selected full models have weak interaction gains, limiting the diagnostic. Do not read the original review-stage status above as a claim that the subsequent pilot remains unexecuted. No broad compression or intrinsic-preference conclusion is established.

## Verdict and scope

A corrected small pilot is justified. This is a different hypothesis from the failed rare-context/tilted-sharing proposal. The previous no-go decisions remain valid for that proposal; they do not test whether an evaluation exposure policy changes the apparent penalty of genuine embedding compression.

The earlier recommendation to proceed directly to an unrelated agentic project should have preserved this unexplored exposure-evaluation alternative. Given the existing data and lack of a new-annotation requirement, screening this corrected pilot is a reasonable next priority. It is not a validated rescue of Idea 2 or a confirmed novel paper.

Use a neutral working question rather than the unproved title 'Logged Data Misprices Embedding Compression.'

## Existing evidence and its exact boundary

The first count probe observed an individual-user/context predictor beating random-shared counts on standard traffic but losing on random exposures. Those comparisons used different table capacities and separately validation-selected user-effect weights. They are exploratory prediction results, not embedding-compression or intrinsic-preference evidence.

Important algebraic limitation: the count predictor has logit score a_(item,context)+b_(user-or-group,context). For a fixed user and context, b is constant across items. Therefore all these models using the same item table induce the same item ranking before finite-precision ties. Switching those saved predictions to within-user AUC cannot test a compression-induced ranking effect. A genuine interaction model, such as p_u dot q_i, is needed.

The E0 protocol expected a growing sharing penalty and a mitigating tilted effect. It was explicitly recorded as exploratory. Do not retrospectively call its negative outcome a preregistered H4 that proves rarity never matters, and do not infer a general activity-concentration result without a separate analysis.

## Corrections to the supplied proposal

1. Randomized exposure reduces a particular item-selection bias under its collection design. It does not reveal context-free intrinsic preferences or represent every possible deployment population. The target population, eligible item pool, user/context weighting and outcome must be defined.
2. Not every public compression benchmark is documented as feedback generated by a recommender with individual user embeddings. GraphHash uses Gowalla, Yelp2018, AmazonBook, Frappe and MovieLens; the proposed upstream-embedding mechanism cannot simply be assumed for all of them.
3. Per-user AUC is invariant to a strictly monotone within-user score transformation and a pure case-control prior shift under fixed class-conditional score distributions. It is not immune to exposure-induced changes in the positive/negative item distributions or eligible user cohort. NDCG also depends on candidate sets and relevance composition.
4. KuaiRand random logs do not provide complete relevance labels for the entire item catalogue for each user. NDCG on observed exposed candidates must not be called catalogue-wide NDCG; unexposed items cannot silently become confirmed negatives.
5. The ratio Delta_random/Delta_logged is unstable or misleading if the logged penalty is near zero or changes sign. Use a paired difference of penalties as primary and report absolute penalties too.
6. Log-loss recalibration requires a separate policy-specific calibration partition, with evaluation afterward. An intercept adjustment or positive-slope calibration preserves item ordering. Successful recalibration does not uniquely prove that calibration caused the original reversal.
7. Training IPS requires justified exposure propensities and support. Inverse item popularity is not automatically the true logging propensity. Defer IPS until a defensible identification/estimation design exists; controlled KuaiRec sampling with known probabilities is a possible later check.
8. A null result does not automatically establish reliable evaluation or yield a publishable paper. It requires enough precision, a stated equivalence margin and a meaningful range of models and budgets. Secondary hypotheses cannot be used as unplanned substitutes until a favorable result appears.

## Targeted prior-art screen

This was a focused web search, not an exhaustive novelty certification.

- KuaiRand explicitly supports evaluation using randomized item exposure: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08696 and https://kuairand.com/
- KuaiRec already shows that density and exposure bias can alter model rankings, in multi-round conversational recommendation. Generic 'selection changes method rankings' is established: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10842
- Unbiased Knowledge Distillation for Recommendation studies bias in teacher/student model compression. It is related rather than a verified exact duplicate of the proposed evaluation-policy interaction: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14729
- CLAX studies embedding compression and changes in click-model rankings, and includes unbiased ranking experiments in search. This is a material neighboring precedent, not a verified identical KuaiRand study: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03620 and https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/m.derijke/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/hager-2025-clax-arxiv.pdf
- GraphHash already studies power/tail users, item popularity and sparse-data settings; those subgroup labels alone are not a new analysis: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17245

An exact novelty claim needs a method-level comparison with these and embedding-compression/debiasing papers. No exact randomized-exposure compression meta-evaluation match was verified in this screen; that is not proof of absence.

## Corrected pilot

1. Use KuaiRand-Pure only. Define a common warm user roster, common item support, tab=1 and the same evaluation period. Report all-user proper loss and the selected AUC-eligible subgroup separately. Pair bootstrap resampling by common user ID across the policies.
2. Fit a genuine matrix-factorization user-item interaction model on early standard exposures. Start with observed positive and negative labels for pointwise prediction; do not silently treat all unexposed pairs as known negatives. This is a diagnostic implementation, not an exact GraphHash/BACO reproduction.
3. At one compressed memory budget, compare the uncompressed reference, random sharing, frequency sharing and a capacity/regularization control. Use three training seeds, the same tuning allowance and early-stop rules, and actual numerical array/mapping bytes. Twelve fits is a possible first grid, not a measured runtime promise.
4. Evaluate the same frozen models on both policies. Report raw and separately calibrated proper loss, within-user AUC on the declared exposed-candidate cohort, uncertainty and attrition. Defer catalogue-ranking claims.
5. For each model m, define Delta_P(m)=E_P[loss(m)-loss(full)]. Primary policy contrast G(m)=Delta_random(m)-Delta_logged(m). Fix user weighting and metric definitions before fitting. For higher-is-better ranking metrics, reverse the penalty sign consistently.
6. A stable nonzero G establishes a policy-dependent measured compression penalty under the declared populations. It does not uniquely identify imitation of the old recommender or intrinsic preference loss. Capacity/regularization and support controls are necessary competing explanations.
7. Add GraphHash/QR and further budgets only after the measured small pilot is discriminating. Do not claim published compression-method rank reversals from only two compressed methods.
8. If the signal is stable, use KuaiRec for a controlled exposure-sampling experiment over a fixed disjoint set of near-complete user-item labels. Keep training cells separate, remove overlapping evaluation cells/events from the large matrix, and hold density/candidate semantics fixed as appropriate. Compare known sampling policies to separate evaluation selection from unrelated dataset differences. Later, Coat can test transfer under explicit-rating semantics.

The listed full proposal implies roughly 190-230 model fits before IPS, depending on BACO inclusion. Job duration, adapter effort and total runtime are unmeasured. Do not assume it fits three days from the word 'small.'

## Dataset distinctions

KuaiRec officially reports a 1,411-user by 3,327-item small matrix at 99.6% density, and a 7,176-user by 10,728-item big matrix. Missing small-matrix cells include blocked videos. It is near-complete within a selected population, not complete universal ground truth: https://kuairec.com/

Coat has self-selected and randomly selected explicit ratings; it is a small complementary dataset, not interchangeable long-view feedback. The original authors provide its archive and propensity-related material: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~schnabts/mnar/

The proposed Evaluation, Human Computation, and Resources track exists in WWW 2027 and includes meta-evaluation/statistical validity. Fit requires a specific Web evaluation contribution, not only three familiar datasets: https://www2027.thewebconf.org/research-track-papers/
