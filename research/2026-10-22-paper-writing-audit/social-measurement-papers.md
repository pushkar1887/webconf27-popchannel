## 1. Behavioral Homophily in Social Media via Inverse Reinforcement Learning: A Reddit Case Study

[Supplied PDF](C:/Users/PUSHKAR/Downloads/WWW2025_PDF_Links_and_Downloader/WWW2025_PDFs/Award_winners/Behavioral_Homophily_in_Social_Media_via_Inverse_Reinforcement_Learning_A_Reddit_Case_Study__2502_02943.pdf)

**Type and actual contribution.** [Certain] This is a measurement-method paper with a substantive Reddit case study. Its contribution is a platform-specific state/action representation, a policy-distance measure of behavioral similarity, and analyses using that representation. Maximum-entropy deep IRL supplies the machinery; the paper does not present a new general IRL algorithm. Its empirical object is 662 selected users from 15 home subreddits and approximately 5.9 million documents/interactions (§§3.1-3.3, Appendix A.3).

**Macro structure and introduction moves.** [Certain] §1 moves from societal stakes to the inadequacy of follower and topical measures, then separates “Unique Challenges,” “Our Contributions,” and embedded “Related Work.” §2 supplies homophily, IRL, and Reddit preliminaries. §3 follows six data-to-measurement stages. §4 organizes the case study into cross-subreddit similarity, behavioral personas, topic-behavior correspondence, and temporal stability. §5 concludes with limitations. Appendices document sampling, classification, clustering, and policy descriptiveness. [Likely] The persuasive move is to make the platform mismatch concrete: Reddit's thematic organization makes topical similarity insufficient for the question being asked, while absent public follower-style ties motivate a different observable. This is more precise than merely claiming that previous work “ignores behavior.”

**Gap-to-design chain.** [Certain] Hierarchical discussions motivate separate thread, root-comment, reply, and received-reply states; agreement labels refine the interaction vocabulary. The resulting policy is a 12-by-6 matrix. State-visitation weighting in symmetric weighted KL divergence reduces the influence of rarely observed states (§§3.4-3.6, pp. 4-6). BERTopic vectors and cosine distance provide a deliberately different measurement of the same users. [Likely] This common-unit comparison makes the proposed construct intelligible before the reader must accept its sociological interpretation.

| Claim being advanced | Evidence and actual scope |
|---|---|
| Behavioral and topical structure differ | [Certain] Figure 3a-b, p. 7, compares the two subreddit-distance matrices; it supports descriptive complementarity. |
| Policies yield interpretable user groupings | [Certain] Figure 3c and Figure 4, p. 7, show action profiles and subreddit compositions for five clusters. Appendix B, p. 13, explains the compromise selection of five clusters. |
| Agreement is common, with informative exceptions | [Certain] §4.3 and Figure 5a,c, pp. 7-8, report Spearman comparisons with Bonferroni correction and investigate the soccer/esports exception. |
| Policies describe observed trajectories | [Certain] Appendix C.2, pp. 13-14, compares each fitted policy with 1,000 random policies; 660 of 662 rank first. This is descriptive fit, not reported held-out predictive validation. |

**Visual and writing lessons.** [Likely] The side-by-side heatmaps carry the central argument; aligned community labels let readers inspect what the new measure changes. The radar plot translates clusters into actions, the stacked bars return those actions to communities, and the anomaly plot supplies a memorable case. Imitate that progression from measurement contrast to interpretable decomposition to targeted exception. Also imitate the explicit null observation: §4.4 reports no significant overall temporal pattern before offering narrower exploratory interpretations. Do not compare the magnitudes of the two heatmap color scales as if they shared units.

**Limits.** [Certain] Author-stated limitations include restricted platform access, dependence on sufficient data for the state/action space, and excluded voting activity (p. 8; Appendix A.2). [Likely] Reviewer inference: similar fitted policies establish behavioral similarity, not preferential tie formation or psychological motives. Random-policy comparisons do not establish necessity over an empirical transition-frequency baseline; stance-classification error and judgment-dependent cluster selection constrain persona interpretation. Language about “intrinsic drivers” and engagement causing convergence exceeds the descriptive evidence presented.

**Reusable two-sentence narrative.** [Likely] “Existing measures observe [available proxy], but [platform structure] prevents that proxy from answering [behavioral question]. We operationalize [missing construct] from [observed actions], compare it with the established measure on the same units, and analyze both agreement and informative disagreement.”

## 2. Towards an Inclusive Mobile Web: A Dataset and Framework for Focusability in UI Accessibility

[Supplied PDF](C:/Users/PUSHKAR/Downloads/WWW2025_PDF_Links_and_Downloader/WWW2025_PDFs/Web4Good_special_track/Towards_an_Inclusive_Mobile_Web_A_Dataset_and_Framework_for_Focusability_in_UI_Accessibility__WWW2025_pdf.pdf)

**Type and actual contribution.** [Certain] This combines formative human research, task definition, dataset construction, and a technical baseline framework. The contribution chain is FIF (Focusability, Information, Functionality), UFP (UI Focusability Prediction), NOS annotations, and GIFT graph modeling. The central empirical resource contains 117,480 components across 2,000 labeled pages (§4, pp. 5-6). [Likely] Its strongest claim to usefulness is that a previously poorly specified accessibility need becomes an operational task; architectural novelty is only one part of that argument.

**Macro structure and introduction moves.** [Certain] After the abstract, §1 introduces the practical problem and previews participant findings. §2 presents the formative study; §3 derives the FIF taxonomy and explains why existing methods do not cover UFP; §4 introduces NOS and its user evaluation; §5 develops GIFT and evaluates three research questions; §6 concludes. Related work is Appendix A, followed by cleaning, dataset examples, task comparison, and external study-detail pointers. [Likely] The introduction works backward from lived failure: general UI understanding misses accessibility requirements; interviews expose stage and granularity issues; a task taxonomy identifies the neglected prerequisite; a dataset and method make that task researchable.

**Gap-to-design chain.** [Certain] Twelve blind/low-vision participants describe non-accessible, over-accessible, and selective-accessible problems. Eight focusability subtasks become a unified component-level prediction task (§§2-3, pp. 3-4). NOS supplies labels absent from conventional UI datasets. Table 3, p. 7, maps each subtask to required relationships. GIFT combines cropped screenshots, text, script attributes, and coordinates with the UI hierarchy; heterophily and depth-specific individuality motivate the choice of graph backbone. [Likely] This requirements-to-representation table is especially reusable: every architectural preference is tied to a particular failure case rather than justified by generic claims about GNN expressiveness.

| Claim being advanced | Evidence and actual scope |
|---|---|
| Existing resources miss the intended accessibility target | [Certain] Figure 1, p. 2; Table 1 and Figure 2, p. 5; Appendix Table 7, p. 12, compare annotated needs with current datasets/tasks. |
| Annotation criteria match user needs | [Certain] Table 2 and §4.2, p. 6, compare simulated pre/post-annotation examples with the same 12 BLV participants plus three sighted participants. This evaluates annotated examples. |
| Suitable relational modeling improves UFP | [Certain] Table 4, p. 8, reports mixed-data macro-F1 83.87 for GIFT+IGNN versus 77.68 for GIFT+MLP, using three random 60/20/20 splits. |
| Contributions differ by input and method | [Certain] Table 5 ablates modalities/graph; Table 6 shows tested MLLMs' near-complete recall alongside low precision/F1, exposing overprediction of focusability. |

**Visual and writing lessons.** [Likely] Figure 1's annotated screenshots teach the distinctions before the taxonomy becomes formal. Its aggregate bars then show that the examples represent a measurable issue. Figure 3, p. 6, clearly separates node information, graph construction, and prediction. Imitate the sequence “user quotation -> named failure -> label rule -> model requirement -> appropriate test,” and preserve stable task identifiers such as T1-T8 across prose, tables, and examples.

**Limits.** [Certain] The authors acknowledge resource-limited labeling, handcrafted attributes, and preference-dependent focus granularity (§§3.1, 4.1, 5.1); there is no dedicated limitations section. Table 2 initially favors TalkBack over annotations for BLV participants on SA aggregation, with interview clarification subsequently changing some opinions. [Likely] Reviewer inference: this discrepancy deserves preservation, not conversion into unanimous validation. A reused small participant cohort and annotated example interfaces do not establish benefits of deployed GIFT. Random splits also do not by themselves establish transfer to entirely unseen applications.

**Reusable two-sentence narrative.** [Likely] “A user study reveals that existing tasks fail at [specific interaction prerequisite], which we formalize through explicit cases and annotation rules. We provide the resource needed to measure that task and test a model whose representation follows those requirements, while separating annotation acceptance from deployed user outcomes.”

## 3. Analyzing User Characteristics of Hate Speech Spreaders on Social Media

[Supplied PDF](C:/Users/PUSHKAR/Downloads/WWW2025_PDF_Links_and_Downloader/WWW2025_PDFs/Web4Good_special_track/Analyzing_User_Characteristics_of_Hate_Speech_Spreaders_on_Social_Media__2310_15772.pdf)

**Type and actual contribution.** [Certain] This is an observational social-media analysis supported by an interpretable modeling pipeline. It studies resharers rather than original authors or targets, distinguishes content types, and extends a prior linear debiasing framework with an explainable boosting machine (DF-EBM). The supplied arXiv v3, dated 27 January 2025, retains a “Conference'17” placeholder header; it should not be described as the final proceedings PDF.

**Macro structure and introduction moves.** [Certain] §1 establishes harms, isolates resharing as a dissemination mechanism, identifies the underexamined actors, and introduces selection bias and latent vulnerability as analytical obstacles. §2 separately reviews hate speech, inverse propensity scoring, and past latent vulnerability. §3 develops clustering, vulnerability modeling, and resharing estimation. §4 specifies data, explainable baselines, and implementation. §5 presents clusters, prediction, aggregate feature curves, content-specific curves, and ablation. §6 separates findings, interpretation, relevance, implications, and conclusion. Appendices A-E provide descriptive statistics, tuning, cluster examples, robustness, and embedding comparisons.

**Gap-to-design chain.** [Certain] Different hateful content types motivate BERTopic/HDBSCAN clustering and Llama-3-assisted cluster naming. Unobserved exposure motivates three propensity proxies and inverse weighting. Past behavior becomes a 64-dimensional BPRMF embedding; a nonlinear additive predictor uses this alongside verified status, account age, posting volume, followers, and friends (§3, pp. 3-5). Its outcome is the observed fraction of reshares that are hateful, with content-specific variants. [Likely] The writing advantage is that methodological steps answer named inferential obstacles before results are shown. The corresponding responsibility is to keep the proxy assumptions visible when describing findings.

| Claim being advanced | Evidence and actual scope |
|---|---|
| A flexible explainable predictor improves fit | [Certain] Table 1, p. 6: RMSE 0.0863 versus 0.0885 for DF-linear, a reported 2.5% relative improvement; §5.2 reports Welch tests across runs. |
| Resharing patterns vary across attributes | [Certain] Figure 1 ranks model feature importance; Figure 2, pp. 6-7, gives centered additive contribution curves. These are model-based associations. |
| Aggregation hides content-specific differences | [Certain] Figures 3-5, p. 8, repeat the same four-attribute display for racist/misogynistic, political, and toxic-rant clusters. |
| Vulnerability adjustment contributes | [Certain] Table 2 and §5.5, p. 7, show only 0.0866 to 0.0863 RMSE improvement over no PLV, explicitly reported as not statistically significant. Appendix D, p. 11, reports separate recommender checks. |

**Visual and writing lessons.** [Likely] The repeated small multiples are the main explanatory instrument: fixed feature order makes differences between content types inspectable. Centering contributions at zero and explaining their relation to the outcome scale help readers interpret direction and magnitude. The progression from aggregate behavior to heterogeneous subgroups offers a stronger story than a single “typical hateful user” profile. Imitate the explicit “Interpretation” paragraph's tentative explanations, but audit each verbal summary against its plotted shape; several displayed relationships are nonlinear.

**Limits.** [Certain] The paper acknowledges unobserved exposure/interest, positive-only interaction data, unstable inverse weights, and possible resource/fairness costs of differential moderation. It has no standalone limitations section. [Likely] Reviewer inference: propensity proxies and latent embeddings do not independently demonstrate exchangeability or removal of confounding. Better RMSE does not validate causal effects, and a non-significant PLV ablation cannot confirm its effectiveness. Recommendations to hide follower counts or remove verification are intervention hypotheses, not tested consequences. Dataset-count and some prose/plot inconsistencies also require checking before borrowing numerical summaries.

**Reusable two-sentence narrative.** [Likely] “Prior research studies [content creators or targets], while [redistribution mechanism] makes another actor consequential. We model that actor with explicit observation assumptions and interpretable, subgroup-specific estimates, separating predictive evidence from causal explanations and intervention proposals.”

## 4. Detecting Linguistic Bias in Government Documents Using Large Language Models

[Supplied PDF](C:/Users/PUSHKAR/Downloads/WWW2025_PDF_Links_and_Downloader/WWW2025_PDFs/Web4Good_special_track/Detecting_Linguistic_Bias_in_Government_Documents_Using_Large_Language_Models__2502_13548.pdf)

**Type and actual contribution.** [Certain] This is a domain-and-language dataset paper with benchmark and error analysis. DGDB supplies expert-labeled Dutch government sentences; the technical evaluation uses existing BERT-family models and generative LMs. [Likely] Its reusable contribution pattern is “a consequential domain has an unmet measurement/data need,” not “a newly named architecture solves governance.”

**Macro structure and introduction moves.** [Certain] §1 defines linguistic bias, explains context dependence, surveys the English/news/encyclopedia concentration, and introduces government documents plus Dutch coverage. §2 distinguishes datasets for text bias, gender bias, hate speech, and bias inside generative models. §3 covers definition, collection, annotation, and characteristics. §4 states four RQs, specifies models/prompts, gives results, and analyzes errors. §§5-8 cover sustainable-development implications, limitations, ethics, and conclusion. Appendices supply the keyword list and translated plots. [Likely] The strongest opening move is definitional discipline: the reader first learns what the label means, then sees why adjacent datasets cannot simply substitute for it.

**Gap-to-design chain.** [Certain] A list of 120 terms in nine categories supports retrieval from post-2010 parliamentary documents. The pipeline produces 42,800 matches and 12,076 candidate sentences before expert annotation (§3, pp. 3-4). Fourteen native-speaking government experts use prohibited, conditionally biased, and context-sensitive guidance; the binary task is formalized separately. Fleiss' kappa is 0.35 on the assessed annotation subset. Three fine-tuned BERT variants, a Dutch hate-speech model, and two named generative baselines test utility. Twelve rare terms comprising 69 instances define the held-out-term evaluation (§4.1, p. 5). [Likely] This deliberately difficult split is the paper's most important protection against equating keyword familiarity with transferable bias detection.

| Claim being advanced | Evidence and actual scope |
|---|---|
| Government/Dutch coverage fills a resource gap | [Certain] Table 2, p. 2, compares task, source, language, size, and agreement; Figure 1, p. 3, makes the acquisition process inspectable. |
| Specialized training works in-domain | [Certain] Table 5, p. 6, reports BERTje F1 0.812 and RobBERT 0.811 under the selected sampling regime. |
| Generalization remains difficult | [Certain] The same table reports BERTje 0.554 on held-out terms, near GPT-3.5 0.559 and GPT-4o mini 0.550; this qualifies the abstract's broad superiority language. |
| Errors depend on labels and terms | [Certain] Figures 3-4 show term-level variation; Figure 5, p. 7, reports 93% correct for unbiased versus 63% for biased instances; Figure 6 supplies LIME examples. |

**Visual and writing lessons.** [Likely] Table 2 argues dataset relevance through comparable dimensions rather than a literature list. Figure 1 separates automated acquisition from human annotation. Aggregate scores are followed by per-term bars, a confusion matrix, and local explanations, progressively answering “how well?”, “for whom/which terms?”, and “what drove this prediction?” Imitate that diagnostic descent. Preserve the unfavorable out-of-domain result as a substantive finding about the limits of the resource and models.

**Limits.** [Certain] Author-stated limitations are access to queryable government data, incomplete seed coverage, and obtaining consensus on bias terms (§6, p. 8). [Likely] Reviewer inference: keyword-conditioned collection and modest agreement bound construct coverage; 69 rare-term instances provide narrow transfer evidence. Monitoring biased wording is not measured improvement in legislation or governance. Source consistency also needs care: Figure 1/Table 2 report 3,632 labels, while §3.4/§8 report 3,747. The p. 5 footnote equating micro- and macro-F1 merely because the task is binary is mathematically invalid; exact metric semantics should be checked before reusing scores.

**Reusable two-sentence narrative.** [Likely] “Existing resources cover [adjacent tasks and languages] but omit [specific domain and construct], so we turn an explicit annotation definition into a traceable dataset. We test its utility in-domain, challenge transfer with held-out concepts, and use errors to delimit what the benchmark supports.”

## 5. Contextualized Counterspeech: Strategies for Adaptation, Personalization, and Evaluation

[Supplied PDF](C:/Users/PUSHKAR/Downloads/WWW2025_PDF_Links_and_Downloader/WWW2025_PDFs/Web4Good_special_track/Contextualized_Counterspeech_Strategies_for_Adaptation_Personalization_and_Evaluation__2412_07338.pdf)

**Type and actual contribution.** [Certain] This is a systematic generation-strategy and evaluation study, using one LLaMA2-13B generator. It tests which community, conversation, and user context helps perceived counterspeech quality, and whether automatic metrics agree with human ratings. [Likely] The methodological comparison is at least as important as the best generation configuration; the paper supports perceived adequacy/persuasiveness, not demonstrated reductions in future toxic behavior.

**Macro structure and introduction moves.** [Certain] §1 moves from toxicity harms to moderation alternatives, manual counterspeech's labor/safety/scalability limits, automation, and two remaining gaps: missing context and inadequate evaluation. §2 separates counterspeech, persuasion, and personalization literature. §3 formalizes generation and desired properties. §4 describes generation factors, automatic evaluation, configuration selection, human design, tests, and power. §5 defines data; §6 separates automatic and human results and compares them. §7 separately discusses generation, evaluation, and limitations; §8 acknowledges support. Appendices give related-work coverage, property definitions, prompts, questionnaires, and successes/failures. [Likely] The dual-gap opening ensures that disagreement between evaluation methods belongs to the planned story rather than looking like post-hoc damage control.

**Gap-to-design chain.** [Certain] Community adaptation uses Reddit fine-tuning; conversational adaptation supplies preceding comments; personalization supplies comment history or a generated user summary (§4.1, p. 3). Thirty-six configurations generate 4,608 responses to 128 toxic comments from 49 threads. Automatic ranking selects best/worst configurations within adaptation/personalization groups plus a baseline. Human evaluation uses 20 centroid-near messages per selected configuration, two contextual-information conditions, and seven within-subject configurations (§§4.2-5, pp. 4-5). [Likely] Explicit factor labels and a staged evaluation contract allow a complex study to remain navigable, although the selected-item scope must accompany the large participant count.

| Claim being advanced | Evidence and actual scope |
|---|---|
| No context factor uniformly improves quality | [Certain] Figure 2, p. 5, compares presence/absence by indicator; Table 1, p. 6, exposes trade-offs across all 36 configurations. |
| Some context improves human ratings | [Certain] Figures 3-4, p. 7, show effect sizes and intervals: BaPrHi improves adequacy and perceived toxic-author persuasion without evaluator context; BaPr improves perceived author persuasion with context. Many comparisons are non-significant. |
| Evaluator context changes assessment | [Certain] Figure 5, p. 8, compares the two conditions, with larger improvements for several initially poorly rated configurations. |
| Metrics can reverse the apparent winner | [Certain] §6.2.3 and Figure 6, pp. 7-8: human-human Kendall tau is 0.62, versus -0.05 and -0.43 for algorithmic-human rankings. |

**Visual and writing lessons.** [Likely] The factor matrix makes the comparison reproducible; aligned effect-size panels make significance and uncertainty visible. Figure 6's connected rankings compress the evaluation disagreement into one inspectable argument. Appendix Table 3, p. 12, adds inadequate, incorrect, meaningless, and toxic generations, explaining why metric improvements need not mean successful counterspeech. Imitate the consistent labels, property definitions, reported nulls, and explicit connection between failure examples and quantitative findings.

**Limits.** [Certain] Authors state single-model/strategy limits, imperfect indicators, crowd representativeness/reliability, and untested longer-term effects (§7, p. 8). The paper reports 2,444 and 2,353 retained participants and no preregistration deviations; those are paper-reported facts. [Likely] Reviewer inference: respondents judge what would persuade someone else; they are not observed toxic authors responding to intervention. A politically focused Reddit sample and metric-based selection of 20 representative items per configuration limit breadth. Stronger contextualization ratings therefore do not establish effective field moderation, and the prediction that larger models will resolve failures remains prospective.

**Reusable two-sentence narrative.** [Likely] “An intervention lacks [relevant context], and its standard evaluation may not measure [desired human outcome]. We systematically vary the context and compare automatic with human assessments, reporting both the configurations that help and the evaluation disagreements that constrain the conclusion.”

## Cross-paper synthesis for the parent report

- [Likely] A contribution becomes legible when the missing object is explicit: behavioral similarity, accessibility focusability, the resharing actor, contextual linguistic bias, or context-sensitive counterspeech.
- [Likely] The most reusable design pattern is a visible dependency chain: problem evidence -> operational definition -> data/representation -> matched evaluation -> bounded conclusion. Inclusive Mobile Web makes this especially explicit through Table 3's task-to-relationship map.
- [Likely] Measurement papers benefit from comparing different views of the same units. Behavioral Homophily compares topic and behavior; Counterspeech compares automatic and human judgments; Government Bias compares familiar and held-out terms.
- [Likely] A good results section changes resolution deliberately: aggregate result -> component or subgroup analysis -> concrete example -> limitation. Repeated figures, stable identifiers, and targeted exceptions help readers follow that progression.
- [Certain] None of these supplied empirical designs by itself establishes all of the downstream social benefits discussed. Preserve the distinction between fitted associations, label agreement, benchmark prediction, perceived user benefit, and actual behavioral or institutional change.
