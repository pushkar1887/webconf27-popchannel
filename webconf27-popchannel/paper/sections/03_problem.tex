\section{Problem Formulation and Preliminaries}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Notation}
Let $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ be the users and items, and let $c_i$ be the number of training interactions of item $i$. We write $p_i=\log(1+c_i)$ for its log-count. A matrix-factorisation (MF) model assigns user $u$ an embedding $\mathbf{e}_u\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and item $i$ an embedding $\mathbf{q}_i\in\mathbb{R}^d$, with $d=64$. We call $\|\mathbf{q}_i\|_2$ the item's \emph{magnitude} and $\mathbf{q}_i/\|\mathbf{q}_i\|_2$ its \emph{direction}.

\subsection{Embedding sharing}
A sharing scheme maps items to buckets, $b:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{B}$ with $|\mathcal{B}|\ll|\mathcal{I}|$, and stores one row per bucket: $\mathbf{q}_i=\mathbf{E}_{b(i)}$ (\emph{single-row sharing}). Two-row schemes such as double hashing sum two rows, $\mathbf{q}_i=\mathbf{E}^{(1)}_{b_1(i)}+\mathbf{E}^{(2)}_{b_2(i)}$. Users are shared in the same way. The \emph{traffic} of bucket $b$ is $C_b=\sum_{i:b(i)=b}c_i$.
Our main scheme is GraphHash~\cite{wu2025graphhash}, which clusters the user--item graph with Louvain modularity optimisation~\cite{blondel2008louvain} at resolution 200. On Gowalla, Yelp2018 and AmazonBook it yields 5{,}806, 7{,}656 and 9{,}804 item buckets (mean 7.06, 4.97 and 9.34 items per bucket; largest 124, 183 and 343), and the shared tables hold 16.4\%, 21.9\% and 13.6\% of the full tables' parameters.

\subsection{Training objectives}
BPR~\cite{rendle2009bpr} minimises $-\sum\log\sigma\big(\langle\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_i\rangle-\langle\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_j\rangle\big)$ over observed items $i$ and sampled items $j$. DirectAU~\cite{wang2022directau} minimises an alignment term and two uniformity terms on $\ell_2$-normalised embeddings $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_u,\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DAU}}=\E\|\hat{\mathbf{e}}_u-\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i\|^2+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\Big[\log\E e^{-t\|\hat{\mathbf{e}}_u-\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{u'}\|^2}+\log\E e^{-t\|\hat{\mathbf{q}}_i-\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{i'}\|^2}\Big],
\end{equation}
with $t=2$ and $\gamma=1$. The distinction that matters here: BPR's loss sees magnitude; DirectAU's loss does not.

\subsection{Scoring modes}
A trained model can rank items in four ways:
\emph{raw}, $\langle\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_i\rangle$;
\emph{cosine}, $\cos(\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_i)$;
the \emph{\crs}, $\cos(\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_i)+\beta p_i$;
and \emph{raw}${}+\beta p$, $\langle\mathbf{e}_u,\mathbf{q}_i\rangle+\beta p_i$.
The \crs{} has the form of Cheng~\cite{cheng2026rankone} (eq.~53), who adds a log-popularity term to dot-product scores on full tables. We use it as an instrument: if sharing erases item popularity, restoring item counts should restore DirectAU's lead (\S\ref{sec:intervention}).

\subsection{Diagnostic quantities}
We measure how magnitude relates to popularity at two levels: $\rho_{\mathrm{item}}=\mathrm{Spearman}(\|\mathbf{q}_i\|,c_i)$ over items and $\rho_{\mathrm{bucket}}=\mathrm{Spearman}(\|\mathbf{E}_b\|,C_b)$ over buckets. For any item-level target $t$ we define the \emph{correlation ratio}
\begin{equation}
\eta^2(t)=\frac{\Var\big(\mu_{b}(t)\big)}{\Var(t)},\qquad \mu_b(t)=\frac{1}{|b|}\sum_{j:b(j)=b}t_j,
\end{equation}
with variances taken over items with uniform weight. We use $t=p$ (log-count variance) and $t=\mathrm{rank}(c)$ (for Spearman correlations).

\subsection{Problem statement}
Table~\ref{tab:problem} shows the observation this paper explains. With full tables, DirectAU leads BPR on all three datasets. Under GraphHash, it falls behind BPR on Gowalla and Yelp2018, and its lead on AmazonBook shrinks by 55\% (from $+1.19$ to $+0.53$\pp). Two prior studies report DirectAU underperforming under hashing~\cite{wu2025graphhash,shiao2025oov}; neither explains why. We ask: \emph{why does sharing remove DirectAU's advantage, and what does sharing erase?}
