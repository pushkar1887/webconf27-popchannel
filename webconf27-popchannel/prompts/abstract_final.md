Embedding sharing cuts recommender memory, but can discard item-level popularity information and change which training objective performs best. Prior work connects embedding magnitudes to popularity and controls it through normalization or item-specific terms. Earlier compression uses random or frequency-aware hashing; recent graph-based approaches group items by interaction structure. Neither line asks what sharing does to the popularity signal. . In our  empirical and analytical diagnosis we  identify a pooling bottleneck: shared embedding norms track bucket traffic (Spearman 0.93–0.94) more strongly than individual-item popularity (0.28–0.52). On Gowalla, DirectAU outperforms BPR with full tables (18.02% versus 16.55% Recall@20), but falls behind under graph-based sharing (7.55% versus 9.37%). We apply a correlation-ratio bound to quantify this representational constraint and use fixed-coefficient interventions to separate bucket calibration from item-specific information. An established, validation-tuned log-popularity correction, applied without retraining, raises compressed DirectAU to 13.30% on Gowalla; item-specific information accounts for 71–76% of the measured gain on two datasets. Corrected DirectAU exceeds equally corrected BPR by 0.83–2.13 percentage points across three datasets with three seeds each, with positive paired-user confidence intervals throughout. The float32 prior adds 3.9–7.3% to embedding-table storage, while accuracy gains favor popular items: tail recall remains below full-table references and catalog coverage contracts.
